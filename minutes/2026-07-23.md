  - **Susannah** - Have we maintained content that would welcome someone building a website on Squarespace or Wix etc.? Users of such platforms may have sustainability goals, but may find the doc too technical or think it’s not for them.

    - **Alex** - some elements remain useful for that audience, but in giving away responsibility for hosting etc. removes those readers a little.

    - **Susannah** - we may be able to influence this group of people by specific inclusion and reference in the guidelines. Call out by tagging e.g full service web hosting

    - **Alisa** - did a workshop at Wix, incentivised internally to make things prettier, rather than more sustainable. Lack of understanding among end users of such services.

    - **Rose** - need to be thorough and speak to all people regardless of what their options are. Can be prone to oversimplification by lumping things together, we can break things out and present options in our writing. Be cognizant of the different levels of influence different readers may have.

    - **Mike** - supporting documentation may have space for this, “Here’s how WSG applies to you, if you’re buying from these companies here’s what you can do”.

    - **Rose** - be clear in general guidelines info. Not everything is something every reader can do, but if you find something you don’t have control over, find guidance on how you can influence third parties. Not all guidelines are for everyone, some may apply to specific roles only - they still must be included. Highlight content by attribute, e.g. “If you host your own infra, look here”. Consider those within the community working on Sprout who may be able to support in this area.

  - **Tim** - Reflecting on Morgan’s Slack post: feeling like optimising images isn’t important enough vs data centres and their massive impact

    - **Rose** - the general public are often worried by AI and its impact, but are less aware of the differences between data transfer, data storage, and data processing etc. Need to be careful about perception of the group by presenting carefully about the use of AI etc. Concentrate on what you can change, rather than what is outside your control.

    - **Alex** - don’t have personal influence over big tech, but where we can reduce the impact of anything, we should. All reductions mount up to something meaningful. W3C holds some sway over web builders, if we can reduce things going over the wire with them, it helps. Other groups have similar aims in terms of standards e.g. ISO, Green Software Foundation. We are not alone.

    - **Mike** - everyone can do something. AI helps in areas such as copy editing and finding accessibility patches etc. Consider return on investment in using AI to create a Drupal patch that improves 1 million websites.

    - **Rose** - experimenting more with the Llama model to produce something bad enough to need to edit (rather than going Claude and getting something too polished that can dazzle).

    - **Sid** - <https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3715275.3732007> How is AI affecting us more widely, cognitive decline etc.? It might be efficient, but what is the wider cost to society? More junk is being created, we need to talk about that too.
