  - [**Conformance**](https://github.com/w3c/sustainableweb-wsg/pull/480): pull request

    - Alex: the old model of conformance was bare-bones; most people won’t be able to conform to the overall document (e.g. they don’t have the remit, etc).

    - The new model sets a minimum baseline of continuous improvement. Provide evidence of progress.

    - Percentage based threshold against “full conformance”. Currently set at 80%. Gives wriggle-room for organisations to pick the most applicable SCs.

      - This should prevent people thinking conformance is impossible, it gives it a framework for achievability.

    - Tzviya : encourage us to review this. Other W3C groups have a lot to tell us about conformance; there are various pitfalls to avoid.

      - See [note ](https://github.com/w3c/wcag3/issues/816)on WCAG 3.0 Conformance, which shows some of the issues. Not all applies (e.g. assertions, but useful for awareness).

    - Jennifier: some issues with conformance generally, but the way this is framed (almost gamefied) sounds like it will help get people on board.

    - Tzviya: we’re avoiding being prescriptive in how people approach this.

    - Jennifer: what conformance levels will we use? (akin to “WCAG 2.2 AA full”, etc)

      - Alex: there are standards like GRI for reporting already; when you implement WSG you can map to one of these existing standards rather than using an overall WSG Conformance Measure Metric.

      - Keep it simple: show evidence of progress against the SCs; if you get towards the “normal max” of 80%, then you can claim conformance to the Specification.

    - Tim: B-labs used 80%-type measures, and had issues that we could learn from.

    - Alex: PR Diff preview is not working

    - Siddhesh: [Challenges and Benefits of a Methodology for Scoring Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.0 Conformance](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GOVPUB-C13-0982fc4f6a6e73c1cd45b5fcf21668af/pdf/GOVPUB-C13-0982fc4f6a6e73c1cd45b5fcf21668af.pdf)

    - Tzviya: open to discussion on what the threshold is (60, 70, 80)

    - Tim: a case-study approach was useful and could apply here, see how successful orgs achieved various

    - Siddhesh: map % to A, AA, AAA or similar? 70% = Level D?

    - Alex: an upper threshold isn’t a negative, it gives a guiding star / aspiration. But, having thresholds (like WCAG) has a pitfall, orgs will often go for the lowest “legal” one they can get away with. Which runs against progress over perfection.

    - Ines: when we have checklists, some items might be simple, others may take years. A recent example - took 9 months to install 1 javascript - due to lack of accountability in the corporate setting. So, an implementer might get to 60% then hits a big roadblock (e.g. change a font) - which can take a year. They are making progress, but its not visible (the progress is behind the scenes).

    - Tzviya: we already agreed not to do levels; likes Ines's suggestion of lowering the magic threshold number.

    - Susannah: Multiple conformance networks? Do we certify the certifiers?

    - Tim: a lot of orgs don’t think in terms of continuous improvement at all.

    - Tzviya: We need to publish, conformance is important. Shadi, an AGWG expert will have important thoughts about it. Hidde is setting up call.

    - Tim: is 80% difficult?

    - Alex: you can exclude SCs if they are not relevant to your org, and that doesn’t count towards your %. It's baked in. It's written in a way that precludes people from throwing stuff out just to improve their score. 

      - [ed] Presumably, instead of 80% of 123 SC, you need 80% of 80 SCs cos your ruled out 43 of them, etc

    - Every SC is clear on what the reporting looks like, how to show evidence.

    - If you are in the EU and subject to the CSRD, the record-keeping aligns with WSG conformance reporting.
