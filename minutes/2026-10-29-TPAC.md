- **Alex summary:** treating conformance different to WCAG (A, AA, AAA). 

  - Our worry: people shoot for meeting “A” and never improve

  - We are trying to go through the process with people working on conformance

  - Can conform at SC level or guideline level or at section level –Partial or full

  - **Emphasis:** continuous improvement (we class as partial conformance)

    - Start where you can and make progress as you can, but if making claims you must first be transparent about what conforming to and second provide evidence of how conforming

  - For purposes of reporting standards – is this the threshold for XYZ policy that applies

    - So fits into global framework w\.r.t. What compliance may be taken up in the future

  - We want people to progress beyond their initial conformance so:

    - In next cycle you must show evolutionary improvement upon what done before and of course show the evidence

  - So:

    - Instead of taking on the whole thing at once, people can start at the best place and then add the next piece and it is improvement

    - Our wish: this is an improvement and relatively easy to start and then also to iterate and improve

  - But people will say “we are compliant” instead of saying partially conformant so

    - We have a minimum threshold

    - Because not everything applies to everyone not realistic

    - So: set percentage to hit in order to make a claim of conformance

    - And: if you can show a WSG doesn’t apply to your org then you can exclude that WSG from your percentage

      - Anti gaming: must show that it doesn’t apply 

  - In conclusion: 

    - Dual model provides balance to help people to show conformance to WSG

**Alex:** Separating the documents, one of the issues we have if we broke the documents into multiple pieces, a lot of the SC and guidelines overlap into multiple categories, and we’d run into, if it applies in more than one section and we’d have a problem if it applies to UX and web dev or infrastructure, etc. The whole point of WSG is holistic specification, and it goes against the grain of what the specification is built around, looking at everything as a whole, the system thinking, and breaking documents apart may run the risk that people prioritise one specification over the other in terms of conformity. E.g. CSS has a lot of documents for individual specifications but the actual out in the wild there is not great uptake for some of them, some are more popular than others. Something like business or product strategy might not get as much uptake from web developers. People talk about things they have not initially considered, looking at the document as a whole makes them think if things apply to them or not, the problem in tech is siloing, departments don’t talk to each other, and runs the risk of defined roles dealing only with what they’re dealing with.
