    - Emma: You’ve talked about horizontal before but can’t wrap my head around it; can you summarise the significance of us being a horizontal is?

    - Tzviya: W3C has 4 horizontal groups: Accessibility, Privacy, Security, Security, Internationalisation. Every specification is reviewed by other groups, and need to make sure specs pass an accessibility review. Sustainability is being elevated to the fifth horizontal. It’s a slow process, it takes time, but becoming a pillar of W3C that we value sustainability in all our specifications. We’re still working on establishing the guidelines-- the way specs are reviewed for each of those other groups varies, it’s slow and human-oriented. We’re not writing a sustainability self-review questionnaire because I’d like for us not to have to be too involved in reviewing other specifications. For example, if a working group writes an API, we’d have to investigate what all those things mean. I’d like for us to write guidance on how to write sustainable applications or incorporate our information into the societal impact questionnaire which is for now optional, but I’d like our work to be integrated with other people’s work. If there is a standalone questionnaire there may be ways to make it more hands-off or help people integrate automated tooling. A lot of our materials and anything in business or policy will not be used at the specification level. Need to establish ourselves as the horizontal and get the word out to the working groups that they have to think about sustainability. The problem with all horizontals is it’s often viewed as a checklist at the end. The specification is mostly written already, they get the feedback from whatever the topic is, e.g. privacy, and people will get frustrated that they need to make changes so it’s an afterthought. This is where the ethical web principles sit at, where the societal impact questionnaire is trying to be.
